The Forum
Where two continents
meet as equals

The Sirocco is a wind born deep in the Sahara. It crosses the Mediterranean and arrives in Europe — carrying warmth, energy, and connection. It has done so for millennia, long before any border or diplomatic protocol existed. The Sirocco Forum takes its name and its spirit from this wind.

We are a boutique, invitation-only annual platform designed to bring European and African voices into the same conversation — not as donor and recipient, not as policy-maker and subject, but as equal partners in shaping the shared challenges of the coming decades.

Our inaugural edition takes place at the College of Europe, Natolin Campus in Warsaw — one of Europe's most distinguished institutions for the study of European affairs. Warsaw is not a coincidence: as Central Europe redefines its role in the EU and seeks new partnerships beyond its traditional axes, Poland offers a genuinely fresh perspective on what Euro-African cooperation could look like.

The forum is built around three pillars — energy transition, trade and investment, and the digital economy — and a firm commitment to youth: approximately 20% of seats are reserved for professionals under 35, seated directly at the main panels alongside ambassadors and senior leaders.

Why Warsaw?
Central Europe is reshaping its energy supply, its industrial partnerships, and its diplomatic role. Poland — the EU's 5th largest economy — is an untapped partner for Africa. The Sirocco Forum activates this connection from one of Europe's most prestigious academic campuses.
Why Africa and Europe as equals?
Africa is not a development challenge to be managed. It is the world's fastest-growing economic bloc, home to the largest free trade area by number of countries, and a continent of 1.4 billion people — the majority of them under 25. This forum treats that reality as an opportunity for genuine partnership, not patronage.
What is the output?
A Joint Communiqué: a concise, factual summary of the key ideas and recommendations from each session, co-signed by participants and distributed to relevant institutions and governments on both continents.

Forum Agenda
Three conversations.
One room. Real outcomes.

Each panel is structured for discussion, not presentations. African and European voices hold equal weight at every table. A young professional under 35 sits on every panel alongside senior figures — not as a token, but as a voice that shapes the outcome.

I
Energy & The Green Transition
Europe needs to diversify its energy supply and decarbonize its industry. Africa holds some of the world's greatest reserves of solar irradiance, wind energy, and green hydrogen potential. This panel examines how both continents can build a shared energy architecture that serves European security and African industrial development — simultaneously and on equal terms. From LNG supply chains today to the green hydrogen corridor of tomorrow, the question is not whether this partnership is necessary, but how to build it fairly.
Green Hydrogen LNG Supply SoutH2 Corridor Energy Sovereignty Renewable Infrastructure Young Energy Professionals
II
Trade, Investment & Nearshoring
The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) is the largest free trade zone in the world by number of countries. Yet European engagement with African markets remains concentrated in a handful of former colonial relationships. This panel challenges that pattern — exploring how Central European firms can nearshore operations to North and Sub-Saharan Africa, how African investors can access European capital and technology, and how the diaspora community in both directions can serve as a bridge for genuine economic partnership rather than one-directional development aid.
AfCFTA Nearshoring B2B Partnerships Diaspora Economy SME Cooperation Young Entrepreneurs
III
Digital Economy & Technology Transfer
Both Africa and Europe are navigating questions of digital sovereignty — who controls data infrastructure, who sets the standards for AI, and who owns the platforms through which citizens and businesses interact. This panel brings together technology companies, policymakers, and researchers from both continents to discuss cybersecurity cooperation, critical raw materials, and how African startups and European technology firms can build lasting joint ventures rather than extractive supply relationships.
Digital Sovereignty Critical Raw Materials Startup Ecosystems Cybersecurity AI Governance Young Tech Leaders
The European Perspective
Strategic autonomy needs new partners
The EU's push for strategic autonomy in energy, technology, and supply chains cannot succeed without Africa. Critical raw materials, renewable energy capacity, and new trade routes all run through the African continent. The Sirocco Forum is where European institutions begin that conversation with African counterparts — not through aid channels, but through partnership frameworks.
The African Perspective
Development on African terms
Africa's engagement with Europe has too often been defined by conditionality, aid dependency, and asymmetric trade rules. The Sirocco Forum creates space for African voices — governments, businesses, researchers, and young professionals — to define what a fair partnership with Europe actually looks like, and to hold European partners accountable to those terms.
The Participants
Every seat matters.
Every guest is chosen.

The Sirocco Forum is not a public conference. Every participant is personally invited based on their ability to contribute to — or benefit from — the conversations in the room. The guest list is carefully balanced between European and African voices, between public and private sector, and between established leaders and the next generation.

🏛️
Ambassadors & Diplomats
Heads of mission and senior diplomatic staff from EU member states, African nations, and Mediterranean countries represented in Warsaw. Their presence validates the forum's strategic weight and enables the direct conversations that formal diplomatic channels often cannot.
💼
Business Owners & Executives
Founders and senior executives of companies with active or potential interests in EU-Africa trade, energy, agri-tech, mining, or digital services. The forum creates direct B2B connections that would take months to build through conventional networking.
🌍
NGOs & Think Tanks
Senior representatives of development organizations, policy institutes, and civil society groups working on Euro-African relations, energy transition, and regional integration. They bring intellectual rigour and long-term institutional memory to the discussion.
🎓
Academics & Researchers
Faculty and researchers from the College of Europe, the University of Warsaw, and partner African universities with expertise in geopolitics, energy policy, African studies, and international economics. Both European and African academic voices are actively sought.
🌱
Young Professionals Under 35
Approximately 20% of all seats are reserved for young entrepreneurs, researchers, and policy professionals from both Europe and Africa. They are not placed in a separate side event — they sit at the main panel tables alongside ambassadors and senior leaders, as equals. The Sirocco Forum is built on the conviction that the people who will implement the decisions of this decade deserve a seat at the table where those decisions are shaped.

The role of local authorities and city-to-city cooperation between African and European municipalities will be the central theme of the Sirocco Forum Edition Two — 2027.
